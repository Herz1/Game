# World & Narrative Bible

## World design mandate
Create a distinct fantasy world whose technology, architecture, religion, economy, travel, clothing, magic and monsters feel connected rather than randomly mixed.

Build the world around one strong unifying material or phenomenon. Examples of valid *types* of anchors: a celestial mineral, memory-bearing crystal, migratory light, ancient weather engine, living root network, tidal magic, or a lost navigation technology. Do not use an existing franchise's named concept.

## Regional structure
Create at least five visually and culturally distinct regions. Each region should have:
- climate and dominant colors
- architecture vocabulary
- local industries/trade
- religious or folk practices
- one political/social pressure
- one characteristic monster ecology
- one musical/ambient identity
- one visual particle/weather motif

## Eight protagonists
Create eight original protagonists. For each define:
- full name
- age range
- hometown
- base job
- visual silhouette
- primary weapon or magical discipline
- strongest personality trait
- emotional blind spot
- surface goal
- deeper need
- personal antagonist or pressure
- relationship to the world's central mystery
- recruitment sequence
- signature battle mechanic
- 6+ portrait expressions
- speech/word-choice notes
- two bonds with other protagonists that deepen over time

Do not make them eight isolated mini-stories. Their conflicts must progressively interlock.

## Main story shape
Use a five-act macrostructure:

### Act I — Separate roads
Introduce protagonists through personal stakes and local problems. Seed repeated symbols, rumors, organizations, materials or historical contradictions.

### Act II — Shared pattern
Party members notice that unrelated regional conflicts share causes or beneficiaries. Establish an antagonist network and reveal partial truth.

### Act III — Cost of the truth
A major victory exposes an uncomfortable fact about the world's prosperity, history, religion or magic. Personal arcs collide with the main plot.

### Act IV — Fracture and choice
The party suffers a material loss, betrayal, social consequence or world-state change. Individual protagonists must choose whether to continue for reasons larger than their opening goals.

### Act V — Convergence
Resolve the central mystery through a final region/dungeon and multi-stage confrontation. Each protagonist must contribute to the solution in a way connected to their arc.

## Antagonists
Build a faction rather than one cartoon villain. Include:
- ideological goal
- material incentive
- internal disagreement
- public-facing identity
- hidden operations
- at least three named recurring figures with distinct methods
- a final antagonist whose motive is understandable even when their methods are unacceptable

## Side quests
Create at least 30 varied side quests across categories:
- local human drama
- monster/ecology problem
- trade/economy dispute
- folklore mystery
- relationship reconciliation
- travel/logistics challenge
- hidden-history investigation
- comic relief with consequences
- optional combat challenge
- multi-region chain quest

At least five side quests should update after later story milestones.

## Writing rules
- concise in-field dialogue
- longer dialogue reserved for important character scenes
- avoid exposition dumps when environment/NPC behavior can communicate the same fact
- each protagonist has distinct sentence rhythm and vocabulary
- humor should emerge from character mismatch rather than memes
- no modern internet slang unless the setting explicitly supports it
- maintain a terminology glossary
- keep all final text localization-ready
