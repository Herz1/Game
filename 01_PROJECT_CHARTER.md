# Project Charter

## Goal
Create an original, complete, polished Japanese-fantasy 2.5D pixel-art JRPG that can be played start-to-finish and whose source, intermediate assets, design docs, and final build are all preserved.

## Product promise
A handcrafted-feeling adventure in which eight travelers from different regions gradually uncover one shared crisis. The player explores layered diorama-like environments, assembles a flexible party, discovers enemy weaknesses, builds burst turns, breaks enemy defenses, experiments with jobs/equipment, and solves both personal and regional problems through main and side quests.

## Scope principle
Completeness beats sheer size. The game must have a real beginning, escalation, final dungeon, final boss, ending, credits, and post-ending save state. Once the complete loop works, expand breadth aggressively.

## Originality rule
Genre inspiration is allowed. Direct copying of existing commercial characters, stories, maps, icons, UI compositions, exact item lists, exact equations, sprites, portraits, logos, audio, or dialogue is not.

## Success metrics
- first 60 seconds look premium and intentional
- player understands movement, interaction and battle basics without external documentation
- one complete battle decision loop is satisfying in under 30 seconds
- no essential menu requires mouse-only interaction
- every protagonist has a recognizable silhouette, personality, combat identity and narrative purpose
- towns contain optional discoveries rather than functioning as menu hubs
- bosses require adaptation rather than raw damage only
- story terms and proper nouns remain consistent throughout
- final build contains no placeholder text or broken critical path

## Asset production strategy
Use integrated Image 2.5 (when available) to create original portraits, expression sheets, key art, monsters, environment concepts and other high-value visuals, while preserving prompts/raw outputs/processed derivatives. Music and SFX may come from properly licensed libraries such as MaouDamashii (魔王魂) when their current terms permit game use; all third-party provenance, attribution and redistribution constraints must be documented and obeyed. A public repository must never become a loose mirror of third-party asset libraries.
